A
Contemporary heritage

Girih: the light moves across the tile

Girih is the strapwork geometry of Herat’s tilework: interlaced star-and-polygon lines drawn with compass and straightedge. Here it becomes a living field. Night lapis ground, ivory type, and a lantern of Herat turquoise that follows the visitor across the pattern.

Signature mark

An eight-pointed girih star (the {8/3} star polygon) around a small octagon. It reads as a compass rose: one centre, many directions.

Typography

Herat, measured
Marcellus for display: a flared, inscriptional serif that recalls carved lettering on stone portals. Hanken Grotesk for reading: open, calm and very legible at small sizes.
Display: Marcellus 400 · Body: Hanken Grotesk 300–600 · Generous tracking on caps labels

Afghan cultural integration

  • Girih strapwork from Herat’s Friday Mosque tilework, rebuilt as a precise line field instead of a printed ornament.
  • The pointed four-centred arch becomes the menu’s reveal shape and the crop for editorial images.
  • Turquoise and lapis taken from tile glazes, used as light rather than as fill.
  • Pattern lives only in the hero, transitions and section dividers. Reading surfaces stay plain ivory.

Hero concept

A full field of girih lines draws itself outward from the centre in about two seconds, then settles. A turquoise light follows the cursor across the strapwork, so the pattern reveals itself only where the visitor looks. On phones, the light follows the finger and drifts slowly at rest.

Navigation

A full-screen ivory overlay that opens through a growing pointed arch, as if stepping through a portal. Page transitions reuse the arch: it closes on the old page and opens on the new one. The current page is marked in turquoise.

Motion language

Lines draw, light moves, arches open. Nothing bounces or spins. Scroll reveals follow the order of construction: guide circle, then lines, then fill. With reduced motion, the pattern appears finished and the arch becomes a cut.

What makes it distinctive

The heritage reference is the most direct of the three, and the most recognisable to anyone who knows Herat. Because the geometry is computed and lit live rather than printed, it reads as precision and craft, not decoration.

Risk to manage

Pushed too far, it drifts towards the “cultural organisation” look the brief warns against. The rule: pattern only where something moves or changes, never as wallpaper behind text.

B
Architectural intelligence

Pishtaq: the site as a set of drawings

The pishtaq is the tall framed portal of Timurid architecture, the kind that fronted the Musalla complex in Herat. This direction treats the website as an architect’s drawing set: limestone paper, graphite lines, compass construction, and a single Badakhshan lapis accent. Strategy shown as structure.

Signature mark

A pointed arch inside its rectangular portal frame, with the construction axis left visible. Structure and opening: a frame that lets you through.

Typography

Built to read
An inverted pairing. Archivo at its narrowest width for towering display lines that behave like elevations, and Newsreader, a reading serif, for long-form text. Red Hat Mono carries sheet numbers, coordinates and labels.
Display: Archivo wdth 62–118, 700–800 · Body: Newsreader · Labels: Red Hat Mono

Afghan cultural integration

  • Proportion, not ornament: the pointed arch and the pishtaq frame set page grids, image crops and the mark.
  • Compass-and-straightedge construction, the method behind Herat’s architecture, shown openly as a graphic language.
  • Lapis lazuli from Badakhshan, mined there for thousands of years, as the single accent colour.
  • The quietest cultural touch of the three. It is felt through structure and light rather than seen as pattern.

Hero concept

Towering condensed type on the left. On the right, an arch is drawn line by line as a construction drawing. Bringing the cursor near the compass centres reveals the circles that generate the arch, with a live coordinate readout. The ratio shown is calculated from the drawn geometry.

Navigation

“Index” opens a drawing-sheet list: every page has a sheet number (A-00 to A-06) and a drawing type, finished with an architect’s title block. Page transitions slide like changing sheets. On hover, the label widens along Archivo’s width axis.

Motion language

Precise and mechanical. Lines are drawn, sheets slide, type widens. Scroll storytelling moves between drawing types: a plan on the Expertise page, a section on About, a detail on Approach. Each page’s opening is a different drawing, so no two pages share a layout.

What makes it distinctive

The most credible and editorial of the three, and the easiest to keep consistent across many pages and content types. Few consulting firms speak in architectural drawings, and the method itself makes the point about rigorous thinking.

Risk to manage

The cultural identity is subtle enough that some visitors may miss it. It needs the arch photography and lapis accent applied with conviction, or it becomes a well-made minimalist site.

C
The SilkCover signature

Tār-o-Pūd: warp and weft

In Dari, tār-o-pūd means warp and weft, and by extension the very fabric of a thing. Afghan carpets are made of thousands of these crossings. Here silk-like threads of gold, madder and indigo run across a fixed warp, passing over and under. Where the visitor reaches in, the threads draw together. Connection is the subject.

Signature mark

Four threads woven over and under into a square knot, layered on a rotated copy so the knot becomes an eight-fold star. Weaving and girih geometry in one symbol.

Typography

Fine threads, strong cloth
Bodoni Moda, a high-contrast optical serif whose hairlines echo silk thread, with italics for emphasis. Red Hat Text for body copy: warm, round, and clear on dark grounds.
Display: Bodoni Moda 400–600, italic for emphasis · Body: Red Hat Text 300–600

Afghan cultural integration

  • Carpet weaving structure (warp, weft, knot) as the system behind layout grids, dividers and transitions.
  • Madder red, indigo and gold, the classic dye palette of Afghan carpets, kept to threads and accents.
  • Carpet gul (medallion) geometry reduced to the woven-knot mark.
  • A Dari phrase gives the system its name internally and can appear once, in About, as the firm’s story.

Hero concept

A dark loom. Threads flow slowly across a fixed warp, crossing over and under it. When the visitor reaches into the field, nearby threads gather towards the point. A visual of a consultancy drawing markets, disciplines and people together. On touch, a drag pulls the threads.

Navigation

Each menu item sits on its own loose thread that pulls taut on hover. Between pages, weft threads sweep across the screen and part to reveal the next page. On the Expertise page the same threads become the lines of an interactive expertise map.

Motion language

Fluid but slow, like cloth rather than liquid. Things gather, tighten and part. Every connection line on the site (maps, process diagrams, the expertise map) is drawn as a thread, so the motif carries meaning wherever it appears.

What makes it distinctive

The most original and memorable of the three. Its central metaphor (connection, things woven together, fabric) fits a consultancy, and it gives the strongest recurring element for interactive diagrams and transitions.

Risk to manage

Silk and dark grounds can drift towards fashion or luxury retail. It needs disciplined editorial layouts on inner pages and plain, credible content to stay a consultancy. It is also the heaviest to build well on mobile.

My recommendation

Lead with C, built on A’s geometry and B’s discipline

Tār-o-Pūd gives SilkCover the one thing the brief asks for most: a signature that people remember and that carries meaning across every interactive feature. To keep it a consultancy, I would constrain the threads to girih angles (multiples of 22.5°) so the motion has architectural rigour, and use Pishtaq’s editorial grid and type discipline on the reading pages (About, Insights).

If you prefer one pure direction, B is the safest for credibility with institutional clients, and A is the strongest cultural statement.

You can also mix specific elements, for example C’s hero with A’s arch menu. Tell me which parts you want and I’ll build the homepage prototype from them.
